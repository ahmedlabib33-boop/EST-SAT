## 1. Mechanics & Motion

### 1.1 Kinematics

**Equations of Motion (constant acceleration):**
```
v = u + at
s = ut + ½at²
v² = u² + 2as
s = (u + v)t/2
```

Where:
- u = initial velocity
- v = final velocity
- a = acceleration
- t = time
- s = displacement

**Example:** A car starts from rest and accelerates at 2 m/s² for 5 seconds.
- u = 0, a = 2 m/s², t = 5 s
- v = 0 + 2(5) = 10 m/s
- s = 0 + ½(2)(5²) = 25 m

### 1.2 Newton's Laws of Motion

**First Law:** An object at rest stays at rest; an object in motion stays in motion unless acted upon by a force.

**Second Law:** F = ma
- Force = mass × acceleration

**Third Law:** For every action, there's an equal and opposite reaction.
- If object A exerts force F on object B, then object B exerts force -F on object A

**Example:** Find acceleration of 5 kg object with 20 N force.
- F = ma
- 20 = 5 × a
- a = 4 m/s²

### 1.3 Friction and Resistance

**Friction Force:** f = μN
- μ = coefficient of friction
- N = normal force

**Types:**
- Static friction: fs ≤ μsN (object at rest)
- Kinetic friction: fk = μkN (object moving)

**Air Resistance:** F = ½ρACdv²
- ρ = air density
- A = cross-sectional area
- Cd = drag coefficient
- v = velocity

### 1.4 Circular Motion

**Angular Velocity:** ω = θ/t = v/r
- θ = angle (radians)
- v = linear velocity
- r = radius

**Centripetal Acceleration:** ac = v²/r = ω²r

**Centripetal Force:** Fc = mv²/r = mω²r

**Example:** Object moving in circle, v = 10 m/s, r = 2 m
- ac = 100/2 = 50 m/s²

### 1.5 Gravitation

**Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation:**
```
F = GMm/r²
```
- G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²
- M, m = masses
- r = distance between centers

**Gravitational Field Strength:** g = GM/r²

**Weight:** W = mg

## 2. Energy & Work

### 2.1 Work

**Definition:** W = F·s = Fs cos θ
- F = force
- s = displacement
- θ = angle between force and displacement

**Example:** 50 N force at 0° over 10 m
- W = 50 × 10 × cos(0°) = 500 J

### 2.2 Kinetic Energy

**Formula:** KE = ½mv²
- m = mass
- v = velocity

**Example:** 2 kg object at 5 m/s
- KE = ½(2)(5²) = 25 J

### 2.3 Potential Energy

**Gravitational PE:** PE = mgh
- m = mass
- g = 9.8 m/s²
- h = height

**Elastic PE:** PE = ½kx²
- k = spring constant
- x = extension/compression

**Example:** 10 kg at 5 m height
- PE = 10 × 9.8 × 5 = 490 J

### 2.4 Conservation of Energy

**Total Energy:** E = KE + PE = constant (no friction)

**Example:** Ball dropped from 20 m
- At top: KE = 0, PE = mgh
- At bottom: KE = mgh, PE = 0
- Total energy conserved

### 2.5 Power

**Definition:** P = W/t = E/t
- Power is energy per unit time

**SI Unit:** Watt (W) = Joule/second (J/s)

**Example:** 1000 J work in 5 seconds
- P = 1000/5 = 200 W

## 3. Waves & Oscillations

### 3.1 Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM)

**Characteristics:**
- Acceleration proportional to displacement: a = -ω²x
- Force: F = -kx (Hooke's Law)
- Period: T = 2π/ω = 2π√(m/k)
- Frequency: f = 1/T = ω/2π

**Energy in SHM:**
```
Total Energy = ½kA² (constant)
KE = ½kA² - ½kx²
PE = ½kx²
```

### 3.2 Wave Properties

**Wave Equation:** v = fλ
- v = wave velocity
- f = frequency
- λ = wavelength

**Frequency and Period:** f = 1/T

**Intensity:** I = P/A = power/area

**Example:** Wave with f = 50 Hz, λ = 2 m
- v = 50 × 2 = 100 m/s

### 3.3 Sound Waves

**Speed of Sound:**
- Air (20°C): v ≈ 343 m/s
- Water: v ≈ 1500 m/s
- Steel: v ≈ 5000 m/s

**Intensity Level (Decibels):**
```
β = 10 log₁₀(I/I₀)
```
- I₀ = reference intensity = 10⁻¹² W/m²

### 3.4 Doppler Effect

**Observed Frequency:**
```
f' = f × (v ± vobserver) / (v ∓ vsource)
```
- Use + for observer moving toward source
- Use - for observer moving away
- Use + for source moving away
- Use - for source moving toward observer

**Example:** Source 100 Hz, observer moving 10 m/s toward stationary source
- v = 343 m/s (sound)
- f' = 100 × (343 + 10) / 343 = 102.9 Hz

### 3.5 Light Waves

**Speed of Light:** c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s

**Electromagnetic Spectrum:**
- Radio waves: λ > 10⁻² m
- Microwaves: 10⁻³ to 10⁻² m
- Infrared: 7 × 10⁻⁷ to 10⁻³ m
- Visible: 4 × 10⁻⁷ to 7 × 10⁻⁷ m
- Ultraviolet: 10⁻⁸ to 4 × 10⁻⁷ m
- X-rays: 10⁻¹¹ to 10⁻⁸ m
- Gamma rays: < 10⁻¹¹ m

## 4. Electricity & Current

### 4.1 Electric Charge

**Coulomb's Law:** F = k(q₁q₂)/r²
- k = 9 × 10⁹ N·m²/C²
- q = charge (Coulombs)
- r = distance

### 4.2 Electric Field

**Electric Field Strength:** E = F/q = kQ/r²

**Potential Difference:** V = W/q = Ed
- W = work done
- d = distance

### 4.3 Current and Resistance

**Electric Current:** I = Q/t
- I = current (Amperes)
- Q = charge (Coulombs)
- t = time (seconds)

**Resistance:** R = ρL/A
- ρ = resistivity
- L = length
- A = cross-sectional area

**Ohm's Law:** V = IR
- V = voltage
- I = current
- R = resistance

### 4.4 Electric Power

**Power:** P = VI = I²R = V²/R
- Measured in Watts (W)

**Energy:** E = Pt = VIt

**Example:** 10 A current, 100 V voltage for 1 hour
- P = 10 × 100 = 1000 W
- E = 1000 × 3600 = 3.6 × 10⁶ J = 1 kWh

### 4.5 Circuits

**Resistors in Series:** RT = R₁ + R₂ + R₃ + ...

**Resistors in Parallel:** 1/RT = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃ + ...

**Kirchhoff's Laws:**
- First Law (Current): Sum of currents entering = sum leaving
- Second Law (Voltage): Sum of EMF = sum of voltage drops around circuit

**EMF and Internal Resistance:** ε = IR + Ir
- ε = EMF
- I = current
- R = external resistance
- r = internal resistance

## 5. Magnetism & Electromagnetism

### 5.1 Magnetic Fields and Forces

**Force on Moving Charge:** F = Bqv sin θ
- B = magnetic field strength (Tesla)
- q = charge
- v = velocity
- θ = angle between v and B

**Force on Current-Carrying Conductor:** F = BIL sin θ
- I = current
- L = length of conductor

**Magnetic Flux:** Φ = BA cos θ
- B = magnetic field strength
- A = area
- θ = angle between B and normal to area

### 5.2 Electromagnetic Induction

**Faraday's Law:** ε = -N(dΦ/dt)
- ε = induced EMF
- N = number of coils
- dΦ/dt = rate of change of magnetic flux

**Lenz's Law:** Induced EMF opposes the change producing it

**Induced Current:** I = ε/R = -N(dΦ/dt)/R

### 5.3 Transformers

**Transformer Equation:**
```
Vs/Vp = Ns/Np = Ip/Is
```
- V = voltage
- N = number of turns
- I = current
- s = secondary, p = primary

**Power Transmission:** Pp = Ps (ideal transformer)

### 5.4 AC Circuits

**RMS Values:**
- VRMS = V₀/√2 (for AC voltage)
- IRMS = I₀/√2 (for AC current)

**Impedance:** Z = √(R² + X²)
- X = reactance (XL for inductor, XC for capacitor)

## 6. Modern Physics

### 6.1 Quantum Theory

**Photon Energy:** E = hf = hc/λ
- h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s (Planck's constant)
- f = frequency
- c = speed of light

**Photoelectric Effect:** hf = Φ + KEmax
- Φ = work function
- KEmax = maximum kinetic energy of electron

### 6.2 Atomic Structure

**Bohr Model:** Energy levels En = -13.6 eV/n²
- n = principal quantum number

**Frequency of Emitted Photon:**
```
f = R∞c(1/n₁² - 1/n₂²)
```
- R∞ = Rydberg constant

### 6.3 Nuclear Physics

**Mass Defect:** Δm = Zmp + Nmn - mnucleus
- Z = number of protons
- N = number of neutrons

**Nuclear Binding Energy:** BE = Δmc²

**Radioactive Decay:** N(t) = N₀e^(-λt)
- λ = decay constant
- Half-life: t₁/₂ = ln(2)/λ

**Activity:** A = λN

### 6.4 Relativity

**Rest Energy:** E₀ = mc²

**Total Energy:** E = γmc²
- γ = Lorentz factor = 1/√(1 - v²/c²)

**Time Dilation:** t = γt₀

**Length Contraction:** L = L₀/γ

## Practice Problems

### Mechanics
1. Object falls for 3 s. Find final velocity and distance.
2. 50 kg person in elevator accelerating up at 2 m/s². Find apparent weight.
3. Car in circular path: v = 20 m/s, r = 50 m. Find centripetal acceleration.

### Energy
1. 100 kg skier slides down 30 m slope. Find speed at bottom (friction neglected).

3. 500 W device runs for 4 hours. Calculate energy consumed.

### Waves
1. Wave with f = 50 Hz, λ = 0.5 m. Find velocity.
2. Source 1000 Hz, observer moving 20 m/s away. Find observed frequency.

### Electricity
1. Find equivalent resistance of 10 Ω and 20 Ω in parallel.
2. 120 V supply, 40 Ω load. Find current and power.
3. Capacitor charges to 5 V in 2 seconds. Find charge if C = 100 μF.

### Modern Physics
1. Photon frequency 5 × 10¹⁴ Hz. Find energy.
2. Nucleus at rest decays, emitting particles. Explain energy source.
